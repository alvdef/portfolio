In school, a friend and I started Nandit, a streetwear brand. It didn't end in what most people would call "success", but it was the first time I built something from scratch and put it in front of real people, and I've been chasing that feeling ever since.

Hi, I'm Álvaro. That's the thread through everything I do: I like to create things from scratch. I think that's why I enjoyed LEGOs as a kid and design as a teenager, and it's why I'm a software engineer. The thing I live for is finding projects to be obsessed with and *learning things by exploring*.

Nandit came from tinkering with my dad's camera. I learnt to edit photos and videos with the Adobe Suite (obviously I paid the license, as everyone should do), and from there it was a short step to designing clothes.

In 2021, the year before university, I discovered coding and I fell in love with it. The challenge was as big as you wanted it to be and I wasn't constrained by anything other than the time spent learning and building. It was like designing but better: the reward came much faster. It was addictive. So I made the obvious decision and enrolled in Computer Science.

Even though the university was teaching me a lot, I kept doing online courses, as I had always done. I learnt web development, which despite not loving it, helped me build a "product mindset": what you build doesn't have to be a terminal tool or a cool technical experiment, it can be something normal people use and enjoy. In the meantime I was reselling clothes and sneakers, because why not.

In my second year I was hired as a research assistant at the university, tutored by Javier Fabra (huge thanks to him for everything), and I kept the position through the rest of my studies, Erasmus year included. First I worked on integrating the Europass credential system into the university under the Certidigital project. I built a beta version of the system, and it taught me a lot about integrating with legacy infrastructure.

After some months I moved to the project that later became my thesis: taking advantage of the variability in AWS EC2 Spot prices to allocate capacity when it's cheapest. I built the whole prediction framework, from a module that captures the price history to a general forecasting model in PyTorch. It was my first complete data product, on the biggest cloud provider, and I got to know its architecture and trade-offs up close. Along the way I worked with Universitat Rovira i Virgili (URV) to integrate the system into their research pipelines, helping them make their deployments tolerate interruptions and move to Spot. I wrote about it in the [lab](/lab/aws-spot/context).

During my Erasmus year in Napoli I had to decide what came after the bachelor's. I had two things clear:
1. I wanted to live outside of Spain for a few more years.
2. I was loving my thesis, particularly the deep learning research.

So when the chance to do an MSc in Data Science at TU Wien came up, I knew it was the right move. Vienna is the antithesis of Napoli: I traded chaos and improvisation for order and structure, and living both extremes made me appreciate each of them. If I'm being completely honest, Napoli is still my favourite of the two.

And here I am, writing this from my room in a Studentwohnheim in the 20th district of Vienna. This year I've focused on the rigorous side of data science (statistical simulation, experiment design) alongside learning German. Both are a personal challenge, as I usually teach myself by building projects out of pure curiosity. Going to class gives me the structure I need for the language and for the theoretical foundations of the field.

Over Christmas break I started using agentic tools like Claude Code, and it gave me the exact same feeling as my very first coding project. The friction is almost gone. Let's keep building.
